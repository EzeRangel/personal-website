## The problem

If you're in RESICO and invoice on your own, you issue and receive CFDI all month. At month's end, those XML files have to become a tax return: separate what you collected from what you invoiced, match payment complements, subtract withholdings.

That draft took me hours. I built Fiscalio because I'm one of those taxpayers.

## The decision

Local-first by default. Your XML files stay on your computer, and Fiscalio never asks for your SAT password or e.firma. Privacy isn't a setting you turn on: it's the shape of the product.

## How it was built

<BlueprintDiagram />

Fiscalio is an Electron app for macOS and Windows. Inside, a Next.js and React app runs on your own computer, with no remote server. You load your XML files; the app parses them, validates them and shows them as invoices you can read. Everything is stored in PGLite, a Postgres database that lives in a file on your computer, with automatic daily and monthly backups.

### AI in the product

The suggestions come from a pattern engine, not an LLM. For each invoice you classify, it stores a snapshot of its features: RFC, SAT product codes, taxes and currency. I chose this because every suggestion can be explained and everything runs offline.

When a pattern keeps landing on the same account, it gains confidence; with enough evidence, it becomes a rule. Correct a suggestion and that pattern loses confidence.

### AI in my workflow

I used AI agents to build parts of the app from specs and phased plans I review at each checkpoint, to write the end-to-end tests and to run the first sales campaign.

## Where it stands

Fiscalio is at v1.0.7, in private beta. No results to show yet: in December 2026 I'm preparing drafts with the first users, using their real CFDI. I've prepared my own returns with Fiscalio since December 2025: the draft that took me hours now takes minutes.

In a local-first app, backups are the product's job. I learned it when my computer broke and I lost seven months of data; automatic backups shipped in 1.0.

What I'd do differently: build a set of test XML files from real cases from day one. Payment complements, credit notes and related CFDI are where the integration breaks.

Next: cancellations and substitutions, and how they adjust the taxes for a month you've already filed.
